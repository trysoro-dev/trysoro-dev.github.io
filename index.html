Trysoro Review (2026): What Soro Does and What It Costs

Trysoro sells hands-off SEO: it picks keywords, writes the article, and publishes to your site daily. The pitch is clear, so the questions should be too.

Short answer firstSoro is aimed squarely at founders who know they need content and have no intention of writing any, and the product is honest about that: add your site, let it learn, let it publish daily. If you have a house style, a compliance reviewer, or a niche where a wrong sentence costs money, an autopilot that ships every day is a liability rather than a feature. The graphs on the homepage are the part to treat carefully, because a rising line labelled started using Soro is a correlation someone chose to screenshot. The promise on the front page

The headline is about getting customers from Google and ChatGPT, and the supporting line says Soro writes, optimizes and posts SEO content proven to rank, all while you sleep. Three stacked statements follow: it finds the keywords that bring buyers, it writes and publishes daily content to your site, and that content appears in both Google and ChatGPT results. The site also displays a rating of 4.9 out of 5 from 1,783 customers. That is the vendor's own figure, reported here rather than verified. What is notable is how little the copy asks of the buyer: no skills needed appears verbatim, which tells you the target reader is not an SEO.

Those Search Console screenshots, read carefully

Most of the homepage is a carousel of traffic charts. Each one shows clicks and impressions climbing, with figures like 76,000 clicks against 865,000 impressions, an average click rate near nine percent and an average position in the eight to nine range, and each carries a marker reading started using Soro. Treat these the way you would treat any customer chart. They are screenshots selected by the vendor, the account histories are not shown, and nothing rules out other changes happening at the same time. Average position around eight or nine with a nine percent click rate is also an unusual pairing, which is worth asking about directly rather than assuming.

How the workflow is meant to run

The site describes three steps and they are refreshingly short. You enter your website link and Soro learns the site in minutes. The SEO autopilot then produces fresh content ideas every day. Finally you publish, with the copy promising ten times more SEO content that ranks on Google and ChatGPT. Two supporting features get their own blocks: keyword selection, described as learning which keywords will make you visible before writing anything, and style capture, described as picking up your brand voice so articles sound like you wrote them. Unlimited blog posts and content ideas are mentioned as part of the offer. What is never described at that level of detail is how the publishing connection is actually made, which matters more than the writing does.

Who this genuinely is not for

Regulated industries first. If a claim in an article can trigger a legal review, daily automated publishing is the wrong shape of tool no matter how good the drafts are. Second, anyone whose traffic already comes from a small set of high-intent pages that need editing rather than expansion; volume does not help there and can dilute what is working. Third, teams with an actual writer on payroll, who will spend more time correcting voice than drafting from scratch. Everyone else, meaning solo founders and small teams with an empty blog and no plan for it, is exactly the customer this was built for. If you recognise yourself in the first three groups, no amount of output quality will fix that mismatch.

Pricing and what the homepage omits

No prices appear before the pricing page, so none are quoted here. The buttons say buy now rather than start free, which usually signals a paid-first funnel rather than a trial, though that should be confirmed on the pricing page itself. Also absent from the front page: which content management systems the publishing integration supports, whether drafts can be held for approval, and what happens to your site connection if you cancel. Those three answers decide whether this is a tool or a dependency, and none of them are on the homepage. Ask before you connect anything to a live site. A tool you can disconnect cleanly is a very different purchase from one holding the keys to your blog.

The parts that matter

Keywords chosen for you

The site says Soro learns which keywords will make you visible, then writes content built for them. You are trusting the selection as much as the writing, which is the real bet here.

Daily publishing, not drafting

The product is sold as an autopilot that posts to your site every day, not as a drafting assistant. That distinction changes everything about how much review you need to plan for.

Voice capture as a feature

One block promises the tool picks up your brand voice and writing style so every article reads like you wrote it. Worth testing against your three best existing posts before you judge it.

Numbers you should verify

A 4.9 rating from 1,783 customers and a wall of traffic charts are vendor supplied. Reported here as claims, not findings. Ask for an account you can look at yourself.

A sane way to trial it

  1. Benchmark before you connect
    Export your current Search Console clicks and impressions for the last ninety days. Without that baseline you will never know what the tool actually changed.
  2. Start on a subdomain or staging site
    Let it publish somewhere that is not your money pages first. You are testing the writing and the keyword picks, not your appetite for risk.
  3. Read the first ten articles properly
    Check factual claims, internal links and whether the voice matches your existing posts. Ten is enough to see the pattern the model has settled into.
  4. Decide on a review gate
    Either accept fully automatic publishing or insist on a draft queue. Choosing deliberately now avoids the awkward discovery that something went live unread.

Questions people actually ask

Is this page run by trysoro?

No. It is an independent review with no connection to the company, and the official site is linked in the footer. Pricing, integrations and feature lists change often, so confirm anything that matters to your decision on the vendor's own pages before you buy.

What does trysoro cost?

The homepage does not show prices, only buy now buttons pointing at a pricing page. No figure is quoted here because none appears in the source. Open the pricing page directly, and check whether there is a trial, since the calls to action suggest a paid-first flow.

Does it really publish to my site automatically?

That is the stated product: the SEO autopilot publishes fresh content daily after you add your website. Which platforms the integration supports is not spelled out on the front page, so confirm your content management system is covered before you plan around it.

Can I trust the traffic graphs on the homepage?

Treat them as marketing. They are vendor-selected screenshots with a marker showing when the customer started, and they do not show what else changed on those sites at the same time. Ask for a reference customer you can speak to instead of reading charts.

Will the articles sound like me?

The site claims the tool learns your content and picks up your brand voice so every article sounds like you wrote it. That is testable in an afternoon: feed it your three strongest posts, generate one article, and read it out loud next to them.
